# Study review · Quiz me from my own notes

[Home](../README.md) · [简体中文](../zh-CN/scenes/study-review.md)

**A review coach Muse runs from your own notes: it quizzes you only on material you have actually read and recorded — one question at a time, no answer up front. When you answer, it marks you against the original: what matched, what you missed, and the exact page to revisit. Questions you half-missed are kept and asked again later.**

## What you get

- **A real question** — drawn from your own reading notes and course material, not a generic question bank; it tests understanding, not recall of trivia.
- **Marking against the source** — your answer checked point by point against the original: which points match (with the passage), which are missing, and which page and line to re-read. No invented scores, no claims the source never made.
- **A weak-spots list** — topics you half-missed are saved, come first next time, and are re-asked after an interval in different words, to see whether you can now give the reason.

## Give this to Muse

Paste the whole block into your Muse. It builds the review flow, then runs it with you.

```text
Build me a study review that quizzes me only from my own material.

Scope: the notes or course material I name. Questions may use only what that material says — do not quiz me on things it never covered, and do not fill questions from your own knowledge.

Each round works like this:
1. Ask one short question and wait for my answer. The question comes alone — no answer attached.
2. When I answer, mark it against the original: which points match (show me where), which point is missing, and which page and passage to revisit.
3. Topics I half-miss go on a weak-spots list and come first next time; after an interval, re-ask them in different words.

Rules:
- Marking follows the source only. If the source does not say it, mark "not in the material" — never improvise on its behalf.
- Keep my answers stored separately from the source material. My answers are mine; the original is the original; never blend the two.

Start now: ask me which set of notes to use, then give me a first question to try.
```

## How it runs

- **Weak spots drive the schedule** — instead of marching through the material from the top, it asks first where you fell short last time. Clearing the weak list counts for more than finishing pages.
- **Depth follows performance** — answer solidly and the next question asks "why"; answer shakily and it steps back to "what" until the base is firm.
