A question from your own notes, with the exact source to revisit if you miss it.

## Sample result

- **Question.** What is the difference between a scheduled check and a change alert?
- **After my answer.** Check against N1. Mark any gap and which line to reread.

## Try it with material you have

Paste the full block below. The input is fictional.

```text
Quiz me on these notes. Ask one short question now and wait for my response. After I answer, tell me what matches the note, what is missing, and which source to reread. Keep the question anchored to the note and do not reveal the answer yet.
N1 | page 2 | A schedule sets when to check. A change alert is sent only if the observed state meets the rule.
N2 | page 4 | The original report should remain attached to a fact so it can be verified.
```

## With a connected tool

Use a small set of reading or course notes. Keep reviews private. After the first question is useful, set a rhythm and carry forward which sources you want to revisit.

## How to check it

The first turn asks a question and waits. After an answer, the correction cites N1 or N2. A gap becomes something to revisit, rather than a fabricated mark.

## Decision boundary

You decide what counts as understood and when to revisit it. Keep the reading source and your answers separable.

## How the cases fit together

The study account schedules review of a personal reading log. The learning-space demo varies topic depth. Start with what you already read, then adjust frequency or difficulty based on your answers.

- Ask from the week’s highlights or the last chapter of a course.
- Reuse a missed question after one week and compare the explanation.
