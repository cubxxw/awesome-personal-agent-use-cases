# Draft workshop · Turn scattered notes into a draft you can edit

[Home](../README.md) · [简体中文](../zh-CN/scenes/draft-from-notes.md)

**A workshop where Muse turns your scattered notes into a first draft: it works only from the notes and past pieces you give it, and every fact in the draft traces back to one of them. Numbers your notes never gave and quotes you have no permission to use are never invented — they land on a separate "to fill" list for you, and enter the text only once you have filled them.**

## What you get

- **A draft you can edit right away** — article, spoken script or video script, written in your voice (give it one past piece as the sample), fully structured, with every fact sourced from your material.
- **An editor-only gap list** — missing evidence, unlicensed quotes, feelings without data: listed separately from the text and never published with it. At a glance you know exactly what to supply next.
- **Outlines before production** — for podcasts and videos, an outline or storyboard comes to you for approval before any full script or recording, so a wrong direction is caught early.

## Give this to Muse

Paste the whole block into your Muse. It builds the workshop, then runs it with you.

```text
Turn my notes into a first draft. Fix two things first: who this is for and where it will appear. Material is only the notes and past pieces I give you — do not go find your own.

Rules:
1. Every fact in the text must trace back to one of my notes. If it cannot, it does not go in the text.
2. Where the material falls short — a missing number, a feeling with no data, a quote without permission — put it on a separate "to fill" list: what is missing and where to get it. Never fill gaps with invented numbers or quotes.
3. Match the voice of the sample piece I give you; with no sample, write plainly, no marketing tone.
4. For audio or video, deliver the outline or storyboard for my approval first, then the full script, then production.
5. Put the draft where I can edit it directly. Keep the gap list stored separately from the text — never ship them together.

Start now: ask me what this piece is and who it is for, then tell me which sections the material can already carry and which it cannot.
```

## How it runs

- **Blanks are a feature, not a failure** — the gap list is your next work list; every item you fill makes the draft one item truer.
- **Approved drafts flow onward** — a draft you have signed off can move straight into a publish package (see the Publish package page) without re-briefing the background.
