An editable draft with its source notes and a short list of gaps to fill.

## Sample result

- **Lead.** A small change made the desk easier to reset [N1].
- **Check with the editor.** N2 mentions less setup time but gives no measurement.
- **Quote pending.** N3 needs permission for direct quotation.

## Try it with material you have

Paste the full block below. The input is fictional.

```text
Draft a short piece for readers setting up a shared workspace. Open with one concrete detail. Keep the voice plain. Tie each factual claim to a note ID. Add an editor-only checklist for missing support or approvals. No invented numbers or attributed quotes.
N1 | field note | Labeling the shelf made it easier to return shared tools.
N2 | participant comment | Setup seemed quicker, no time recorded.
N3 | interview excerpt | Personal story can guide structure, quote permission pending.
```

## With a connected tool

Choose the material and a past piece that represents your voice. Keep the source-linked draft editable. For audio or video, make an outline or shot list first, then review before recording or rendering.

## How to check it

The draft uses N1 as the example, treats N2 as an observation, and leaves N3 as a permission check. A draft with blanks is valid.

## Decision boundary

Review facts, attribution and the audience before sharing. Save the editor notes outside any public copy.

## How the cases fit together

The podcast and video examples both turn a rough idea into a work product an editor can revise. A script, outline or shot list is its own deliverable. It can be checked before final production.

- Use interview notes for an article, or one idea plus constraints for a scene plan.
- Keep one approved source draft to feed later edits or adaptations.
