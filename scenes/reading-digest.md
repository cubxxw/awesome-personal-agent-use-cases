# Topic digest · Only what matters to this topic

[Home](../README.md) · [简体中文](../zh-CN/scenes/reading-digest.md)

**A short digest Muse filters by topic for you: you name a topic and a few sources, and each issue carries only the new items that truly belong — at most three, each with the original link and why it earned its place. Duplicates are merged, unreadable sources are reported honestly, and when there is nothing new it says "nothing this time" instead of padding with old news.**

## What you get

- **A short list** — three items at most by default, all published inside the window and genuinely on-topic, each saying what it is, why it is worth your time, and where the original lives.
- **A source-status line** — which sources published nothing and which could not be read, visible at the top. "Published nothing" and "could not be read" are different facts and are written differently.
- **Automatic dedupe** — the same original forwarded ten times appears once, credited to its earliest source.

## Give this to Muse

Paste the whole block into your Muse. It builds the digest, then runs it with you.

```text
Build me a topic digest.

First, fix three things with me: the topic (one sentence, as specific as we can make it), which sources to watch, and the cadence (daily or weekly) with its time window.

Rules for every issue:
1. Include only items published inside the window that genuinely match the topic — at most three. Each gets: title, one line of content, why it belongs, the original link and its publish time.
2. Items pointing at the same original merge into one, credited to the earliest source.
3. Put a source-status line at the top: every source marked "updated", "no update", or "unreadable". Never guess an unreadable item's content, and never write content for a headline you could not open.
4. If nothing qualifies, send one line — "nothing this time" — instead of loosening the bar to fill the page.

Start now: fix the topic and sources with me, run the first issue by hand, and set the schedule only after I approve the bar.
```

## How it runs

- **A fixed bar, no padding** — the digest earns its keep by missing nothing important and carrying nothing useless. A short issue is not a failure; a padded one is.
- **Swap the topic, keep the format** — a project you are on, a course you are taking, a field you track: a new topic is a new digest in the same shape.
