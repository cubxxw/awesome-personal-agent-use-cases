# Morning action list · What actually needs me today?

[Home](../README.md) · [简体中文](../zh-CN/scenes/daily-plan.md)

**A morning action list Muse lays out for you every day: from your calendar, due items and pending replies it picks what truly needs you today — one main thread, at most three items, each with a start time, a next step and what it is blocked on. Things waiting on other people sit in their own area. In the evening the same list closes out: done, carried over, or dropped.**

## What you get

- **One morning list** — a main thread (the single most important thing today) plus at most three items to actually do. Hard times are placed first, the rest ordered by due date. Anything without a date does not take a slot today.
- **A waiting area** — items stuck with someone else (awaiting a reply, a delivery) are listed separately: out of your attention, never lost.
- **An evening close-out** — at night you return to the same list and mark each item: done, carried over to a named day, or dropped. Yesterday's items never roll silently into today.

## Give this to Muse

Paste the whole block into your Muse. It builds the list, then runs it with you.

```text
Build me a daily action list — generated in the morning, closed out in the evening.

Material: the calendar, to-do sources and pending-reply list I name. Use only what you can actually read; if a source fails, mark on the list what is missing — never pretend you saw it.

In the morning (at the time I set):
1. Set the main thread first: pick the single most important item of the day and pin it on top.
2. Then lay out at most three things that truly need doing today: hard times get a start time, due items get their deadline, and each one states its next step and what it is blocked on.
3. Items waiting on someone else go to a "Waiting" area, saying who and what we are waiting for.
4. Anything with no date, or not needed today, stays off the list.

In the evening (at the time I set):
5. Return to the same list and ask me about each item: done, carried over (to which day), or dropped.
6. Carried items become candidates for tomorrow; dropped items close for good — never present them again the next day as if new.

Start now: ask me which calendar and to-do sources to connect, what time to send the morning list and what time to close out, then run today's list for me.
```

## How it runs

- **One list, morning and evening** — the list you plan with is the list you close with; whether the day moved is written in the evening marks.
- **Broken sources speak up** — "the calendar could not be read" and "nothing is scheduled today" are completely different statements, and the list keeps them apart.
